# Analytical Findings

## Staffing and CMS Ratings

Higher staffing levels were clearly associated with better CMS ratings.

| Total HPRD | Facilities | Overall Rating | Staffing Rating |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| < 3.0 | 3,818 | 2.36 | 1.91 |
| 3.0–3.99 | 6,138 | 2.86 | 2.83 |
| 4.0–4.99 | 1,455 | 3.67 | 3.85 |
| 5.0+ | 406 | 4.23 | 4.31 |

The correlation between Total HPRD and Staffing Rating was approximately **+0.534**, while Total HPRD and Overall Rating was approximately **+0.334**.

## Staffing and Quality Outcomes

Short-stay ED utilization declined as staffing increased:

- <3 HPRD: 12.86
- 3–3.99: 12.11
- 4–4.99: 11.27
- 5+: 10.70

Rehospitalization was also lower in the two highest staffing groups, although the relationship was not monotonic.

Facility-level correlations between staffing and adverse utilization outcomes were generally weak.

## RN Staffing

RN HPRD showed the most consistently negative association with the four adverse utilization measures examined.

However, all correlations were weak (|r| < 0.2).

## Conclusion

Higher staffing is much more clearly associated with better CMS ratings than with utilization outcomes.

These results represent **associations, not causal relationships**.
